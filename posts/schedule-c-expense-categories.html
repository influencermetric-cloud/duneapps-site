Schedule C expense categories: every line on the 2026 form, and what goes on it

Many lists online still show last year's form. The 2026 Schedule C splits the interest line in three. Here is every category on it, what goes where, and how to sort a year of receipts so the totals drop straight onto the form.

By Nahid SaleemUpdated 21 November 20268 min read

Checked against the 2026 Schedule C, the draft 2026 instructions, the 2025 instructions and Publication 334 on 16 November 2026.

Key takeaways
  • Schedule C has 25 expense lines in Part II, numbered 8 to 27b, plus line 30 for a home office and Part III for the cost of stock you sell.
  • New for 2026: interest is split into 16a mortgage, 16b vehicle loan and 16c other. Many lists online still show the old 16a and 16b.
  • Anything with no line of its own goes in Part V by name and totals to line 27b. Software and bank fees go here. A mobile phone goes here or on line 25.
  • Your own health insurance and retirement contributions do not go on Schedule C. They go on Schedule 1, lines 17 and 16.
  • For each receipt the IRS wants the payee, the amount, proof of payment, the date and what it was for. Sort them by line as they come in and the totals are done by January.

#The test every line shares

Before the lines, the rule they all sit under. Publication 334: "To be deductible, a business expense must be both ordinary and necessary. An ordinary expense is one that is common and accepted in your field of business. A necessary expense is one that is helpful and appropriate for your business." It adds that an expense "does not have to be indispensable to be considered necessary," and that when something is partly personal, you separate the two and deduct only the business part.

The categories below decide where a deduction goes, not whether you get it. Put a legitimate expense on the wrong line and the profit at the bottom is the same. Put a personal expense on any line and it is wrong everywhere.

The income side is line 1, gross receipts, and it takes everything the business earned whether or not a form reports it. A payment app or marketplace has to send a 1099-K only when payments "total over $20,000 in more than 200 transactions," and a client has to send a 1099-NEC only from $2,000 in 2026. Below those, the income is still yours to report.

#What changed on the 2026 form

The 2025 form had two interest lines: 16a for mortgage and 16b for everything else. The 2026 form has three, and the draft instructions list them under What's New: "New Line 16b, Vehicle loan interest. Use line 16b to report the business-use portion of your vehicle loan interest." Other business interest that used to go on the old 16b now goes on 16c.

Line 16b applies under either car method. If you take the standard mileage rate, the interest is not inside the rate; you claim the business share of it on 16b on top. The personal share may be deductible on Schedule 1-A instead, but the same dollar cannot go on both. Which car method suits you is its own question, worked through with a calculator in standard mileage rate vs actual expenses.

Two other changes touch line 9 and line 11. The mileage rate changed on 1 July, so the draft instructions tell you to multiply January to June miles by 72.5 cents and July to December miles by 76 cents, as covered in the 2026 rate guide. And the threshold for sending a 1099-NEC to a contractor rose from $600 to $2,000.

#Where the awkward expenses go

Most costs fit a line without thought. These are the ones people get stuck on.

ExpenseWhere it goesWhy
Mobile phoneLine 25 or Part V, business share onlyThe form has no phone line. Line 25's instructions cover a home landline only. Choose one place and keep to it every year
Software and subscriptionsPart V, by name, to line 27bNo line of its own. Part V takes "all ordinary and necessary business expenses not deducted elsewhere"
Bank feesPart V, to line 27bPublication 334 lists bank fees among other expenses you can deduct
Platform or marketplace feesLine 10, or Part V by nameThey are fees you paid. Check whether the income figure on your 1099 is before or after them; if before, the fees are a deduction you have to claim
A laptop or tool under $2,500Part V, if you elect the de minimis safe harborPublication 334: amounts under the safe harbor "should be included as other expenses in Part V". Otherwise depreciate it on line 13

Your own retirement contributionsSchedule 1, line 16Not line 19, which is for employees
Sales tax you collectedIn line 1, then line 23The instructions say collected sales tax goes into gross receipts too, then out again as a tax you paid

Some costs have no line because they are not deductible at all. Publication 334 lists among them charitable contributions, club dues, entertainment, fines and penalties paid to a government for breaking the law, political contributions, and personal, living and family expenses.

#Three examples: a driver, a freelancer, a seller

A delivery driver

Most of the deduction is line 9: business miles at the two 2026 rates, plus business parking and tolls. The business share of the car loan interest goes on 16b. The business share of the phone bill goes on line 25 or in Part V. If an app takes a fee from each payout and the income figure on its tax form is before that fee, the fee is a deduction too. That is four or five lines out of twenty-five, and the delivery mileage calculator does the biggest one.

A freelance designer working from home

Line 8 for a portfolio ad, line 17 for the accountant, line 18 for postage and office supplies, line 30 for the home office under the simplified method, and Part V for each software subscription by name. A new laptop goes on line 13, or in Part V under the de minimis safe harbor if it cost $2,500 or less and the election is made.

An online seller

The stock is not a supply: it goes through Part III, cost of goods sold, which reaches line 4. Packaging goes on line 22 and postage on line 18. Marketplace fees go on line 10 or in Part V. Every sale goes in line 1, 1099-K or not, and any sales tax the seller collected goes in line 1 and back out on line 23.

#How to sort receipts so the totals are done

The IRS describes what each expense record has to show: "Your supporting documents should identify the payee, the amount paid, proof of payment, the date incurred, and include a description of the item purchased or service received that shows the amount was for a business expense." On organizing them, its advice is one line: "organize them by year and type of income or expense."

The Schedule C lines are the types. So the routine is short:

  • One folder a year, and inside it one folder for each line you actually use.
  • File each receipt on the day, with the line number and a few words on what it was for. A receipt shows the payee, the amount and the date; the purpose is the part only you know.
  • Once a month, check the card statement against the folders. The statement proves payment; the receipt proves what was bought.
  • At year end, total each folder. Each total is a line on the form.

Thermal receipts fade, so photograph them while they can still be read. Do you need receipts for tax deductions covers when a bank statement is enough and the $75 rule, and how long to keep receipts covers when you can throw the folders away.

Questions people ask

What are the expense categories on Schedule C?

Part II of the 2026 Schedule C lists them as lines 8 to 27b: advertising, car and truck expenses, commissions and fees, contract labor, depletion, depreciation, employee benefit programs, insurance, interest (mortgage, vehicle loan, other), legal and professional services, office expense, pension and profit-sharing plans, rent or lease (vehicles and equipment, other property), repairs and maintenance, supplies, taxes and licenses, travel, deductible meals, utilities, wages, the energy efficient commercial buildings deduction, and other expenses. Business use of your home goes on line 30, and the cost of goods you sell is worked out in Part III.

Where do I put my phone bill on Schedule C?

There is no phone line. The line 25 instructions deal only with a home landline: the base rate of the first line into your home is not deductible, but extra business costs and a second line are. For a mobile phone, deduct the business share of the bill either on line 25 or as a named item in Part V, Other expenses, and put it in the same place every year.

Where do software subscriptions go on Schedule C?

The form has no software line. The instructions for Part V say to include all ordinary and necessary business expenses not deducted elsewhere and to list the type and amount of each separately, so list each subscription by name there. The Part V total goes on line 27b.

What changed on Schedule C for 2026?

The interest line now has three parts: 16a for mortgage interest paid to banks, 16b for vehicle loan interest, and 16c for other interest. The draft instructions say the business share of car loan interest goes on 16b whether you use the standard mileage rate or actual expenses. The standard mileage rate is 72.5 cents a mile to 30 June 2026 and 76 cents from 1 July, and the threshold for sending a 1099-NEC to a contractor rose from $600 to $2,000.

Does my own health insurance go on Schedule C?

No. Line 14 is for benefit programs for your employees. The draft 2026 instructions say not to include on line 14 what you paid for yourself as a self-employed person, and point you to Schedule 1, line 17, which can cover health insurance for you, your spouse and dependents even if you do not itemize.
