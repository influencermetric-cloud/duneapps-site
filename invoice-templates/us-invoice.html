Invoices

Free invoice template for US freelancers and small businesses

No federal law tells you what an American invoice must show. That is freedom and a trap: what decides whether you get paid on time is what your client's accounts payable team needs to see.

By Nahid SaleemUpdated 15 November 20262 min read

Checked against IRS: What kind of records should I keep and the IRS Form 1099-NEC FAQ on 15 November 2026.

Key takeaways
  • There is no federal invoice format. The IRS treats your invoices as records of gross receipts, kept with your books.
  • Sales tax is state law. Whether you charge it, and at what rate, depends on the state, often the county and city, and what you sell.
  • For payments in 2026, a client only has to send a 1099-NEC at $2,000 or more. They ask for your taxpayer number on a Form W-9, not on the invoice.
  • What gets invoices paid: a unique number, the dates, the payment terms, a clear description, the total, and exactly how to pay.

#What to put on it, and why

Because no federal rule sets the contents, this list comes from what gets an invoice approved and paid, and from what the IRS expects your records to show:

Sales tax

Whether a sale is taxable, and at what rate, is decided by each state, and in many places the county and city add their own rate. Many states do not tax most services. If you do collect it, show it as its own line and keep your permit number to hand. The fill-in version applies one rate to the lines you tick as taxable.

The 2026 change to 1099s

For payments made after 31 December 2025, a business only has to report what it paid you on Form 1099-NEC once the total reaches $2,000, up from $600. It asks for your taxpayer number on a Form W-9, so there is no reason to print a Social Security number on an invoice. The income is reportable whether a form arrives or not, which makes your own copies of every invoice the record that counts.

Questions people ask

What should an invoice include in the US?

No federal law sets a list. In practice: your name or business name and contact details, the client's name, a unique invoice number, the invoice date, the payment terms and due date, a description of the work, quantities and rates, any sales tax, the total, and how to pay. Keep a copy: the IRS lists invoices among the documents that show your gross receipts.

Do I have to charge sales tax on my invoices?

It depends on the state, and on what you sell. Sales tax is set by each state, with county and city rates on top in many places, and many states do not tax most services. Check with the state's department of revenue for each state you sell into. PaidSnap does not fill in a US rate for that reason: the right one depends on where the sale happens.

Should I put my Social Security number on an invoice?

There is no need. A client who has to report what they paid you asks for your taxpayer identification number on Form W-9. For payments made in 2026, they only have to file a 1099-NEC once they have paid you $2,000 or more in the year, up from $600.

How long should I keep copies of my invoices?

The IRS's general period is three years from when you filed, six if income was underreported by more than 25%, and seven for a claim for a loss from worthless securities or bad debt. Many people keep invoices longer because they also prove when a client paid.
